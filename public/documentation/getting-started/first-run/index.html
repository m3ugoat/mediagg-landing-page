Your first minutes

AndroidiOS

What Mediagg plays, and the three ways to put something into it.

Mediagg is a player for media you already have. It provides no content of its own — no catalogue to sign up to, nothing to buy. What it does is put three very different libraries behind one player:

  • Files on your device — the music and video it already holds, and any folder you point it at
  • Your own media server — Jellyfin, Subsonic, Plex, Emby or Audiobookshelf
  • Anything on the open web with an address — podcasts, radio stations, playlists

They share a library, a queue and a player, so a podcast episode and an album track can sit in the queue together and play one after the other.

The three ways in

The first screen you see is Get started, and it offers exactly three things. They map onto the three libraries above.

OptionWhat it is for
Add a subscriptionFollow a podcast by name or by its address
Add a serverJellyfin, Subsonic, Plex, Emby or Audiobookshelf
Add a playlistAn m3u file on this device, or a station list from the web

You do not have to choose one. Most people end up with all three, and nothing about adding one closes off the others.

Nothing is added for you. Mediagg starts genuinely empty — there are no suggested podcasts and no sample content. Until you add something, Home has nothing to show, which is why Get started is what you land on.

Where things go afterwards

Whatever you add, it lands in the Library, filed by what kind of thing it is. A podcast goes under Subscriptions, a server under Media servers, a station playlist under Subscriptions too — on its own Providers chip.

Once there is something in the library, Home becomes the screen worth opening: it puts what you were in the middle of, what has just arrived, and a few things to try at the top of the app.
