What the full edition adds

A directory, not just a search box

Personal can search for a podcast you already know the name of. Full is the edition you open when you don't — and want to go looking.

Radio

Live radio, wherever it broadcasts from

Thousands of stations, browsable by country and category, with station artwork instead of a wall of text. Tap one and it plays through the same player as everything else — same controls, same lock screen, same cast button.

Favourite the ones worth coming back to and they sit with the rest of your library, not in a separate app you forget you installed.

TV

Live channels, beside your own video

A TV tab of live channels that opens in the full-screen player your own films already use. Watch it on the phone, or send it to the television with the same cast button — Chromecast, or any DLNA renderer on the network.

Your Jellyfin films and a live channel end up in one queue, because as far as the player is concerned there was never a difference.

The rest of what Full carries

🎮

Games

Five of them, played against your own music — the tracks, artists and artwork already on the device. No new library to build, no download.

🌍

Discovery that starts near you

Radio, TV and podcast directories all open on your own country first, and let you travel from there.

🧾

Ad-supported

Promotional banners appear in the app's lists — never over your own library while it is playing. The Personal edition is the one without them.

🛠️

Crash reporting

Faults report themselves, so they get found and fixed rather than waiting for someone to describe them. Personal reports nothing.

Would you rather none of that reached out? Mediagg Personal is the same app with the advertising and analytics left out of the build entirely — and with radio, TV and the games gone as the trade. See the Personal edition →

The same in both editions

Everything else is simply the app

There is one codebase and one set of screens. Every feature below behaves identically in Mediagg and Mediagg Personal — a fix to the player, the queue or the library reaches both at once.

🎵

Local media, properly organised

Everything on your device, sorted the way a music app should sort it — songs, albums, videos, and a real file browser with breadcrumbs and storage volumes.

🗄️

Your own media servers

Sign in to Subsonic, Navidrome, Airsonic, OpenSubsonic, Jellyfin or Audiobookshelf — several at once — and browse albums, films and shows with artwork, search and offline copies.

🎙️

A serious podcast player

Subscriptions, queue, inbox and downloads, with chapters and follow-along transcripts, automatic downloads, OPML import and export, and gpodder.net sync.

📚

Playlists that are yours

Create, rename, reorder and delete them in the app, or import the M3U playlists you already have. Add anything from any screen.

🎚️

Playback that behaves

Variable speed, skip silence, a sleep timer, full queue control with swipe actions, and lock-screen and notification controls that work.

🎤

Lyrics, in time

From an .lrc file beside the track, from the file's own tags, or from your server — timed line by line wherever the source says when a line is sung.

📡

Casting without brand loyalty

Chromecast, and every DLNA/UPnP renderer on the network. A built-in media relay means files on your phone reach the device as readily as tracks on your server.

🔍

One search, everything at once

Your device, your subscriptions and every signed-in server queried together, in one list — with a chip to scope it down when you know where to look.

⬇️

Downloads that stay put

Keep what you want on the device and play it with no signal at all. Every screen fills from its saved copy first, so losing the network never blanks it.

🎨

Make it yours

Reorder or hide every home-screen section, choose a bottom bar or a side drawer, grid or list and how many columns, in light, dark or true-black.

🚗

Android Auto, Wear OS, widgets

Browse and control from the dashboard, your wrist or the home screen, with the same library underneath and no second setup.

📊

Statistics and backup

See what you actually played, by year, computed on the device. Export the whole database, or your subscriptions as OPML, to a file you choose.
