`Subscriptions` holds everything you follow — podcasts, the station playlists you have subscribed
to, and single streams you pasted. Four chips along the top ask four different questions of it.

| Chip | What it lists |
|---|---|
| `Podcasts` | Your podcast subscriptions, as a grid of artwork |
| `Episodes` | Every episode of those podcasts |
| `New` | Episodes you have not dealt with yet |
| `Providers` | Stations and streams — everything that is not a podcast |

`Podcasts` is where a fresh install lands. The chip you last used is remembered, so the section
reopens where you left it.

## Podcasts and Providers

These two list subscriptions rather than episodes, and they are split because they do not look alike.
A grid of podcast artwork is scannable; a station playlist dropped into it is a row with nothing to
show. So stations and pasted streams live on their own chip.

Both offer `Refresh all`, `Add a feed`, and a switch between `Show as list` and `Show as grid`.

Empty, `Providers` explains itself: `No stations or streams yet. Playlists you subscribe to from a
provider, and addresses you paste, show up here.`

## New, and what it means

`New` is the inbox: **episodes you have never dealt with**. Every row there carries a `New` badge.

Something leaves the inbox when you deal with it, and there are four ways:

- **Queueing it** — putting something in the queue *is* dealing with it
- **Playing it** — anything played joins the queue, so this is the same thing
- `Remove from inbox`, from the episode's own menu
- `Mark as played`

Empty, it says `Nothing new. New episodes will show up here as your subscriptions publish them.`

> **Only podcasts have an inbox.** A radio station has no episodes to arrive, so nothing of that kind
> ever appears on `New`.

Pull down on any of the four to refresh. See
